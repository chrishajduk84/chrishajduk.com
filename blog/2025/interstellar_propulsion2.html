Interstellar Propulsion - Part 2

Date: June 6th, 2026
Author: Chris Hajduk

In the previous article, we discussed the concept of massless interstellar propulsion. The main point I wanted to make was that while no practical implementation of this technology currently exists, these theoretical concepts have been explored and are feasible within the laws of physics. Using the momentum of light or stray solar particles is a well established method of propulsion in scientific literature.

In this article, I want to suggest the novel concept of a wakefield solar engine.

Using wakefield acceleration, it is possible to generate extremely high electric fields over short distances, which can be harnessed for propulsion purposes. However, the energy required to do so is extremely high, while not being completely massless. In the last article, I provided the force-power conversion ratio calculations for photon-based propulsion. For standard wakefield acceleration, calculating exact efficiency numbers is much more complicated.

As mentioned, wakefield acceleration is not free. This report by Williams provides a hypothetical wakefield solar engine concept, where a pre-existing plasma source is already present. Additionally, the power requirements suggested in the paper requires 10MW! This is not a small amount. This is the total output of 3x industrial Tesla Megapack battery systems or 1000x Kilopower units. As a result, we need some that is more power efficient, since lifting that much mass to orbit, let alone installing it as part of a spacecraft is infeasible with present day technology.
